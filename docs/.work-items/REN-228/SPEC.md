# REN-228 — Seller Settlement Documents & Contract Validation

## Status

`BLOCKED` pending the external tax/document decision and the first-real-cycle dependency. The bounded engineering design is ready, but application changes must wait until the governance blockers are explicitly resolved.

## Linear decision and scope

REN-228 contains four independently gated tracks:

- Track A: make the existing regular seller payout statement a reliable operating control.
- Track B: define regular-order commission-invoice plumbing only; do not invent GST, numbering, or tax-invoice semantics before CA/Registerkaro guidance.
- Track C: route a cadence/content comparison of the existing half-monthly mechanism against Terra Luna §3.5 to Business/Legal or Registerkaro.
- Track D: validate the first real generated statement after a real payout cycle executes.

This implementation covers Track A and the Track B design artifact/plumbing boundary. Tracks C and D are external or future operational checkpoints and are not represented as completed by code changes.

## Repository evidence

- `src/lib/finance/payouts.ts` calculates regular payout cycles, writes line items, and populates `statementUrl` only after Razorpay execution, manual payout instruction generation, or manual completion.
- `src/app/api/finance/payouts/[cycleId]/statement/[brandId]/route.tsx` renders the regular statement PDF from the cycle summary and payout line items, but currently does not enforce that the requested brand belongs to the cycle or that the statement is execution-ready.
- `src/components/pdf/brand-payout-statement-template.tsx` already exposes gross sales, commission, returns, claims, holdback, TDS, net payable, transaction status, and line items.
- `src/components/dashboard/general/finance/payouts-workspace.tsx` exposes the statement link when `statementUrl` exists.
- Corporate settlement statements are already persisted in `corporate_settlement_statements`, generated by `corporate-documents.ts`, and downloaded through a protected corporate route. This is a reference mechanism, not a reason to merge regular and corporate tax semantics.
- No regular-order commission-invoice mechanism was found in the repository; corporate invoice/document flows are separate.
- REN-203/204/205/206 are formal dependencies for correct payout amounts, eligibility/payment-state gating, holdback behavior, and execution safety. The current branch contains their implementation/spec work, but the real statement remains execution-gated.

## Engineering design

### Track A — regular seller payout statement

1. Keep the existing payout-cycle summary and PDF template as the source path.
2. Harden the statement route with finance access control and strict cycle/brand membership checks.
3. Require a statement-ready execution state (`completed`, `awaiting_manual_confirmation`, or an explicitly generated manual instruction) before exposing a seller statement; calculated/approved cycles must not look like paid statements.
4. Preserve the current line-item waterfall and contract-control metadata. The statement must identify cycle, payout date, brand, gross, commission, deductions, TDS, net payable, payment method, execution status, and transaction reference when available.
5. Keep generation idempotent because the statement is derived from immutable cycle/line-item snapshots; do not create a second settlement table for regular payouts.

### Track B — commission-invoice target design

Create a task-local design artifact describing the future regular-order commission-invoice mechanism, including required source data, trigger, storage/reference, access boundary, and relationship to REN-225 invoice numbering. The implementation must stop short of tax-specific content, GST fields, legal classification, and numbering changes until the CA/Registerkaro conclusion is available.

### Tracks C/D

Record the review request and first-real-statement validation as operational follow-ups only. Engineering must not draft the legal conclusion, execute a production payout, or mark BIZ-19 validated from a test PDF.

## Safety boundaries

- No payout execution, production data mutation, historical backfill, cadence change, or tax-document classification change.
- No seller-facing access without finance authorization and cycle/brand ownership validation.
- No commission invoice is treated as a GST tax invoice by default.
